# Knowledge Management Primer

A personal knowledge management (PKM) system is a set of practices and tools for capturing, organizing, and retrieving information that matters to you. The goal is not to store everything — it is to reduce the friction between encountering something worth keeping and being able to use it later.

## Why Most Systems Fail

The common failure mode is not disorganization. It is capture friction: the system requires too much effort at the moment of capture, so nothing gets captured. Notes sit in browser tabs. Highlights accumulate in a reading app nobody opens. The system becomes a source of guilt rather than a resource.

A second failure mode is retrieval friction: even when items are captured, they cannot be found when needed. Search fails because the terminology at capture time differs from the terminology at retrieval time. Organization schemes made sense when the system was small and break down as it grows.

## The Core Loop

The systems that work share a common structure:

1. **Capture** — get the thing into the system with minimal friction; classification decisions come later
2. **Process** — review captures in dedicated time; file, discard, or escalate each item
3. **Retrieve** — find things via search, links, or structured review, not by remembering where they are

The insight is that capture and processing are optimized differently. Capture needs to be instant and frictionless. Processing can be deliberate and structured, because it happens in dedicated time.

## The Role of Automation

Modern tools can automate significant parts of the processing step — classifying captures by type, extracting key concepts, creating cross-links between related items. When automation handles obvious decisions, the human work focuses on the non-obvious ones: what does this mean, how does it connect to what I already know, what should I do with it?

This is the appropriate division of labor in a human-AI knowledge system.
